ClauseGuard — Product Requirements Document

Version 2.0 · Single deployment, single release · Draft 28 September 2026

1. How to Read This Document

This is the acceptance source for ClauseGuard. A behavior ships when a Must requirement's done-when checks pass. The verdict rules, the citation rule, the tenancy walls, and the eval gates are not loosened to meet a date. There is one release, one priority scheme (Must / Should / Next / Won't), and no separate phases. Should ships in this release if the Must set is met and time allows; it does not move to a later phase. Next is designed for and not required for this release. Won't is explicitly out of scope.

The system is single-tenant on day one. The data model, the isolation tests, and the admin panel support creating additional tenants at any time without a schema change or a deployment change — this is a Must requirement, not a future phase.

2. Product Summary

ClauseGuard is a policy-compliance system for a large contract portfolio. Compliance, procurement, and audit staff maintain a versioned set of internal rules. The product checks the in-force contracts against those rules, continuously as the portfolio and the rules change, and on demand when someone asks.

Two surfaces do the work. Ask answers a question about one contract family immediately, or answers a portfolio question from findings already stored. Sweep judges active rules across the in-scope portfolio as a resumable background run. Portfolio-wide conclusions are reads over a finished sweep. They are not a search across every page performed while the user waits. Evidence is a prose span or a table cell. A cell keeps the row and the column it came from.

The user is deciding whether the organization's own rule is met. The product is not deciding whether a clause is market, whether a negotiation should move, or whether a signature should be collected.

3. Buyer and Problem

The buyer has a live portfolio large enough that no person can re-read it when a policy changes. Typical holdings include vendor masters, amendments, statements of work, and order forms spread across business units and systems of record. The people who need the answer are compliance officers, procurement operations staff, and internal auditors. Many of them are not lawyers. They need a result they can check against the page, and a record they can show later.

Today the work is a manual search through repositories and inboxes, repeated before an audit or after an incident. Results vary by who searched, the search terms they remembered, and whether they opened the amendment or only the master. There is rarely a record of what was searched and what was ignored.

The product is valuable when it can do four things reliably:

  • Judge the current in-force text, including the amendment that overrides the master.
  • Treat a missing clause as a finding when the rule requires the clause and the pages were actually read.
  • Leave a contract unchecked when the pages were not read, rather than calling it compliant.
  • Replay, a year later, who asked, which text was in force, which rule version was used, and which model pipeline answered.

A fluent answer that is sometimes wrong is a worse outcome than a smaller set of checked contracts. People will act on the answer. The requirements below are written to make an unsupported conclusion hard to ship and hard to display.

6. Principles

01 No unsupported conclusion. A compliant or non-compliant verdict is stored only when two independent gates both pass: a relevance grade on the retrieved evidence, and a citation check confirming the proposed span or cell matches stored text exactly. Either gate failing alone forces Unchecked. There is no third path.
02 Portfolio answers are stored findings. A question across the portfolio reads a completed sweep. A new portfolio-wide question starts a sweep. It does not sample a few chunks and present them as the portfolio.
03 The in-force text is the text that matters. Precedence is an attribute the customer sets. The finding records the version set that was judged.
04 Two walls, both tested. Tenant isolation and business-unit isolation are release gates, tested even while only one tenant is active, because a second tenant can be created at any time. A prompt inside a contract cannot move either wall.
05 History is immutable. Rule edits, model upgrades, and new file versions create new judgments. They do not rewrite yesterday's answer.
06 Unchecked is a result. Failed OCR, incomplete coverage, weak retrieval relevance, and failed citation checks stay visible. Dashboards never fold those families into the compliant count.

8. Design Point

The design point is the scale the capacity test must pass before this release ships. A tenant that grows past it does not change architecture — the same stack scales by adding Celery workers, Qdrant shards, and Postgres/Neo4j resources — but the capacity test is repeated at the new size before that tenant is trusted to run on it.

In-force families per tenant5,000
Average pages per family (capacity planning)30 pages, ~150,000 pages total
Active rules per tenant50
Business units per tenant10
Concurrent interactive users per tenant10
Tenants on the deployment1 active; admin panel supports creating more at any time; isolation tested with 2+ tenants regardless
Steady-state intakeTens of new or replaced documents per day

5,000 families × 50 rules = 250,000 pairs. Unchanged pairs are not re-judged (FR-SWP-02); the capacity test reports model judgments separately from reused pairs.

Document roles: master, amendment, statement of work, order form, exhibit, other. Contract types are tenant-defined; suggested defaults are vendor master, NDA, statement of work, order form, other.

Coverage is sufficient only when every page of every in-force document has indexed text and every detected table has a parsed grid. A failed or missing page makes coverage insufficient for every rule on that family. A page whose prose was read and whose table failed still supports prose citations (FR-VER-09); absence or a number from the failed grid does not.

10. Verdicts, Evidence, and Confidence

A conclusion is valid only when the product can show the clause, or show that every in-force page was indexed and the required clause is absent. Retrieval must also grade as relevant before generation runs at all (section 12.6). If either the relevance grade or the citation check fails, the family is Unchecked.

SituationVerdictEvidence
must_include, verified span satisfies the ruleCompliantCitation
must_include, coverage sufficient, no satisfying spanNon-compliantAbsence record
must_not_include, verified span matches forbidden topicNon-compliantCitation
must_not_include, coverage sufficient, no matching spanCompliantAbsence record
Numeric/duration/notice rule, one normalized value, comparator agrees or disagreesCompliant or non-compliantCitation, extracted value, unit, threshold
Winning document self-conflicts; two documents share top precedence and disagree; winning span cannot be normalizedAmbiguousEvery competing citation
OCR, extraction, coverage, or pipeline failureUncheckedReason code, failed pages when known
Retrieval relevance grade is incorrect, or citation/tenant/unit/schema check failsUnchecked or AmbiguousGuardrail reason. No invented span.

The comparator owns numeric, duration, and notice decisions after extraction. The model may propose a span, a cell, and a normalized value. It does not get a veto over the comparison.

High = coverage sufficient, relevance grade correct, citation check passed, comparator agrees on a single value. Medium = citation passed with a stored caveat (e.g. weak section label). Low = coverage near the failure line or retrieval support weak, never used to decorate an unchecked result.

10.3 Tabular Evidence

A cell is evidence of the same kind as a sentence. The answer names the cell, the row header, and the column header (full header path for multi-level headers); the viewer highlights that rectangle. A detected table that cannot be split into rows and columns is table_unparsed. The product then refuses two moves: it does not declare a clause absent (the term may be inside the unread grid), and it does not pick a number out of a flattened line that used to be a row.

11. Workflows

11.1 Stand up a tenant and load a portfolio

  • A tenant admin creates the tenant via the admin panel. No region prompt. Additional tenants can be created the same way at any time.
  • The admin creates business units and invites users (email/password, role assignment from section 4).
  • An operator submits a manifest and files, or drops files into the watched folder, or a system submits the same payload via the API.
  • The job page shows per-file status. Rejected and failed files are listed with reasons.
  • The operator opens a family, confirms role and precedence, and confirms which documents are active.

11.2 Publish a rule and sweep

  • A policy owner creates a typed rule, writes the statement, sets applicability.
  • The owner may try the draft on one family — writes an answer trace, not a finding.
  • Activation shows the number of families to be queued; owner confirms.
  • A sweep run opens (cause: rule_activation). Progress counters move. A worker restart continues the run.
  • On completion, the findings queue shows violations, compliant rows, ambiguous rows, and a separate unchecked list.

11.3 Review a finding

  • A reviewer filters the queue to one business unit and one critical rule.
  • On a citation finding: opens the highlighted page or table cell (with headers), marks confirmed or false_positive.
  • On an absence finding: reads the versions and pages searched, confirms or marks false positive.
  • On an unchecked family: sees the failed pages; no compliant action available. Remediation is to replace the file.
  • A risk acceptance requires a reason and expiry date, recorded on that version pair only.

11.4 Ask

  • An analyst asks about one family; answer returns within the latency bar with a citation, or unchecked.
  • An analyst asks for a table value (e.g. liability cap); answer cites one cell with row and column headers.
  • A portfolio question maps to a completed sweep → answer lists findings and names the run.
  • No completed sweep covers it → product starts a job, withholds a portfolio verdict.

11.5 Export for an audit

  • An auditor opens a completed run, checks the unchecked count and pipeline version, exports CSV and PDF.
  • The packet includes the legal notice, scope, and citations or absence records.
  • Exporting a running sweep through the default action is refused. A chosen partial packet is marked Partial on every page.

12.6 Verdicts and Guardrails

IDRequirementPriorityStatement / Done-when
FR-VER-01Closed verdict setMustOnly compliant, non-compliant, not_found, ambiguous. UI labels not_found as Unchecked. Done: no surface emits a verdict outside this set.
FR-VER-02A clause-based conclusion cites a real spanMustQuote, document version, page, character offsets, section label if detected. Quote must be a verbatim substring of the stored derivative. Done: a non-substring quote is rejected by the citation check.
FR-VER-03Absence allowed only with sufficient coverageMustAbsence evidence requires sufficient coverage (section 9). Record lists versions and pages searched, no fabricated quote. Done: an absence verdict on a family with a failed page cannot be stored.
FR-VER-04Coverage/relevance/citation failure is uncheckedMustVerdict is not_found when OCR fails, extraction is empty, coverage is insufficient, the relevance grade is incorrect (FR-VER-10), or the citation check fails. FR-VER-05Ambiguous only when precedence does not decide itMustHighest-precedence document with a normalizable value wins; lower-precedence text is overridden context, not a second verdict. FR-VER-06Guardrail failure downgrades, never retries loosenedMustCitation, tenant, business-unit, or schema check failure → not_found or ambiguous with the guardrail reason. FR-VER-07Confidence bandsMusthigh / medium / low, never "probability." Auditors can open components: coverage, relevance grade, citation check, comparator agreement. Low/ambiguous cannot be bulk-dispositioned. Done: a bulk action on a filter including a low/ambiguous finding is rejected.
FR-VER-08A table answer cites one cellMustCell text as the quote, plus table id, page, row/column index, header labels. A flattened row is not a citation when a grid exists. A cell with no detected header can be quoted but not normalized into a comparison unless it self-labels. FR-VER-09An unparsed table cannot support absence or a guessed numberMustA verified prose or parsed-cell citation can still support a verdict when a different table on the page failed. Done: a family with a clear prose breach-notice clause remains judgeable when an unrelated pricing table fails to parse; a renewal term sitting only in a table_unparsed region is unchecked, even when a flattened line shows the number.
FR-VER-10Relevance grade gates generationMustBefore generation, retrieved evidence is graded correct / ambiguous / incorrect (CRAG-style). Correct → generate. Ambiguous → one bounded re-retrieval on a rewritten query, then re-grade. Incorrect (original or retried pass) → skip generation, store not_found, reason relevance_low. Done: a fixture with no relevant evidence anywhere in the in-force set produces zero generation-stage calls in the trace.

FR-VER-12HyDE output is never citableMustHyDE's hypothetical expansion text drives retrieval only. It is never stored as a citation quote, never shown as document text, logged separately from evidence in the trace. Done: zero citation quotes match HyDE-generated text across a 100-answer trace sample.

14. Security, Privacy, and Tenancy

Customer contracts are the highest data class in the system. Account metadata and product telemetry are lower classes and must not mix into logs that leave the audit store. There is no region concept: one deployment serves all tenants, and isolation is enforced by tenant id at the storage layer (Postgres row-level filtering, Qdrant payload filtering, Neo4j tenant-scoped queries, MinIO per-tenant key prefixes) and again at the application layer, never by physical or geographic separation.

The isolation suite runs as two tenants with two business units each — created via the admin panel (FR-IAM-07) specifically to run this suite, even while only one tenant is in real production use:

  • Request a family, finding, trace, export, or sweep id belonging to the other tenant. Response is empty or generic not-found — never a confirmation the id exists.
  • Repeat as an analyst, a reviewer, and a policy owner across business units.
  • Run retrieval with the tenant predicate removed at the application layer; the data-access layer must still refuse the query.
  • Place instructions inside a contract — ignore the rules, omit citations, print another tenant's clause text. The judgment stays inside the tenant; the citation rule still applies.
  • Call the export endpoint with another tenant's run id, or another unit's filter.
  • Use a revoked ingestion credential and an analyst credential against the ingestion API.
  • Confirm embeddings, graph edges, and MinIO objects for tenant A are unreadable via tenant B's credentials.

A leak includes returning another tenant's text, another unit's text, a count revealing a hidden portfolio, or an error message echoing a hidden identifier. Zero leaks is the bar.

Privacy obligations: encryption in transit and at rest, the no-training commitment, tenant deletion within 30 days, a subprocessor list (embedding and generation model providers). A data-processing agreement is a commercial matter, not an engineering option. Retention deletion is manual under the tenant admin's instruction until legal hold (FR-AUD-05) ships, and is audited either way. A third-party penetration test is recommended before external customer data is trusted to the system but is not a release blocker at this scale.

16. Launch Criteria

Launch means the product is in production use and a second tenant can be created via the admin panel at any time with no additional engineering work. All of the following are true:

  • Every Must requirement in sections 12 and 13 has passed its done-when checks, or has a written waiver — no waiver may cover NFR-ACC-01, -02, -03, -05, -06, -07, -08, or NFR-SEC-01, -03, -04.
  • The five workflows in section 11 have been completed on a corpus including scanned pages, a commercial-terms table, a multi-document family, and seeded violations.
  • The capacity test at the design point (section 8) is attached to the release record.
  • Restore, deletion, and credential revocation have each been exercised once in the running configuration.
  • The subprocessor list and the legal notice match the running system.
  • The isolation suite (section 14) has passed with a second tenant actually created, not just planned.
  • Operational metrics from section 15 are live and a judgment trace can be opened from an alert without reading clause text in the log tool.
